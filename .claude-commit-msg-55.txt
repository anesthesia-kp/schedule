DEMAND — a stacking list, LAST MATCH WINS (§9)

  §9: "Daily. Weekly on certain days, certain days/week or month, weekdays, weekends,
  holidays, every combination of the above, and more." One setting cannot express "two on
  weekdays, one at the weekend, none on holidays", so demand is a LIST of rules per shift.

  Rules are read top to bottom and the LAST one that matches a date decides it. General
  rule first, exception after. The editor numbers them, marks which one wins, and lets you
  reorder — because the order is the entire meaning of the list, and the suite proves it:
  move `daily` below `holidays` and Labor Day flips from 0 to 2.

  Frequencies: every day · weekdays · weekends · named days of the week · holidays. Each
  carries how many people that day needs, and 0 is a legitimate answer.

  A SHIFT WITH NO RULES IS NOT DEMANDED AT ALL — which is not the same as "0 people
  needed" (§9: "Not all shifts need a person every day"). demandOn() returns null rather
  than 0, the preview shows a dash rather than a zero, and the editor says so in words.

  §48: "N per month" is deliberately ABSENT — the owner deferred it. §21 already ruled its
  shape (an obligation the month owes, with suggested dates), and a stacking day-rule cannot
  express "any two days, you pick" without naming them. The suite asserts it is not there,
  so nobody adds it by accident without reading §21 first.

A LIVE 60-DAY PREVIEW

  §9 asks for this and gives the reason: the failure mode is silent. A rule that quietly
  matches nothing looks exactly like a rule that works. Sixty day-cells show the resolved
  need, holidays outlined, with a summary saying how many days actually ask for anybody and
  how many matched no rule at all.

HOLIDAYS — computed federal dates, editable on top (§49)

  A PORT of the auction's computation, NOT a verbatim lift: the auction returns week indices
  because it reasons in weeks, and the schedule reasons in days. The definitions and the
  nth-weekday / last-weekday helpers are the auction's; the return type is not.

  Eleven US federal holidays, not the auction's seven — the auction's list is trimmed to the
  ones that shape bidding. Add or remove any of them.

  THE REAL DATE IS USED, NOT THE OBSERVED ONE. When a holiday falls at the weekend the
  federal observed day shifts to Friday or Monday; a hospital runs on the real day. That is
  a guess about this group's practice, so it is stated on the page, weekend holidays are
  flagged in the list, and the override list is how it gets corrected.

  §19 warns against two sources for one answer, and computed-plus-overrides is exactly that.
  Allowed here on one condition, which is built and tested: EVERY OVERRIDE IS VISIBLE — a
  removed federal holiday shows as "you removed this", an added day as "you added this".
  Never a silent difference between what the code computes and what the calendar says.

★ TAB-THROUGH ENTRY — the thing that made 92 shifts impossible

  Saving a time fires a Firestore snapshot → renderAll() → renderCatalog(), which rebuilt
  the whole list. A rebuilt <input> is a NEW element: focus dies, and the anti-autofill guard
  at the top of the page re-arms it readonly through a MutationObserver, so the next
  keystroke goes nowhere. Tabbing down the catalog was impossible. Same shape as defect 12.

  Two wrong fixes were tried before the right one, and both are recorded because the next
  person will reach for them:

  1. Restore focus after the rebuild. Not enough — the element being tabbed TO is destroyed
     before focus ever arrives, and the restored one comes back readonly.
  2. Skip the rebuild when document.activeElement is inside the list. ALSO NOT ENOUGH, and
     this is the subtle one: on Tab, Chromium fires `change` AFTER blur, so at the instant
     the save triggers the re-render, activeElement is momentarily <body>. The guard misses
     by a hair and the symptom is that Tab appears to do nothing at all.

  The fix is to track editing as a STATE with a debounced exit: focus landing anywhere in
  the list means "still editing", and only a settled departure ends it and triggers the
  rebuild. While editing, only the DERIVED text updates in place — the duration appears, the
  counts move, and the keyboard is never taken away.

NOTHING BLOCKS ENTRY

  The half-set confirmation is gone, per the owner's instruction. alert() on the add path is
  replaced with a toast. A half-set shift is still worth knowing about — it looks finished
  and reasons like blank — so it is COUNTED at the top and flagged on its own row, and never
  put in the way of the next keystroke.

  The summary is now the single place missing configuration is tracked: how many shifts have
  times, how many have a site, how many have demand rules, how many are half set.
