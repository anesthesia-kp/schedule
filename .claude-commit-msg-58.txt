Build 58 (admin) — Stage 2: COVERAGE against demand. Closes defect 6.

DECISIONS §9. Admin only; staff untouched at 27. No rules change. Read-only page.

WHY

  Defect 6: "dashCoverage is meaningless — denominator is every shift × capacity × every
  day. Structurally near-zero." With 92 shifts in the catalog the percentage said nothing
  about whether the month was staffed; it mostly reported how big the catalog was.

  Demand rules (build 55) made a real denominator possible for the first time: WHAT WAS
  ACTUALLY ASKED FOR.

THE NUMBER NOW MEANS SOMETHING

  need  = the sum of what the demand rules ask for, day by day, shift by shift
  have  = what is actually assigned against that, capped at what was asked

  A shift with NO demand rules contributes nothing to either side. §9: "Not all shifts need
  a person every day… A shift with no rules is never demanded and never counts as
  uncovered." It is not being asked for, which is a different thing from being short, and
  the old denominator's central mistake was conflating them.

  The suite pins this with a catalog of three shifts where one — "Never" — has no rules:
  the denominator comes out at exactly days × 3, and "Never" never appears as a gap.

TWO STATES THAT ARE NOT 0%

  This is the part worth keeping. "Nothing has been filled" and "nothing has been asked
  for" are different statements, and only one of them is a problem. So:

  · NO SHIFT HAS DEMAND RULES → the page says so in words, says explicitly that this is NOT
    the same as being fully covered, points at the Shift Catalog, and shows a dash rather
    than a percentage. The dashboard tile says the same.
  · RULES EXIST BUT MATCH NO DAY THIS MONTH → called out separately, with a pointer to the
    60-day preview, because that is precisely where a rule that quietly matches nothing
    becomes visible. It does NOT read as 100% covered.

  Both are asserted. A page that showed 0% for the first, or 100% for the second, would be
  worse than no page at all.

THE BOARD

  Gaps listed by date and shift — asked for, assigned, short by how many — with holidays
  marked. Over-staffed days are hidden by default but always COUNTED in the summary, so
  they are never invisible; a switch shows them.

  The page writes nothing. The suite asserts zero writes of any kind across the whole run,
  not just zero auction writes.
